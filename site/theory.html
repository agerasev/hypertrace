1. Three spaces, one ray

A position is scalar-first P = (w, X) ∈ ℝ⁴. Curved coordinates are normalized by R; physical sectional curvature is K/R².

SpaceKPoint constraintCK(t)SK(t)
Hyperbolic−1w² − |X|² = 1, w > 0cosh tsinh t
Euclidean0w = 1; R = 11t
Spherical+1w² + |X|² = 1cos tsin t
BK((w,X),(z,Y)) = wz + K X·Y
CK² + K SK² = 1,   C′K = −K SK,   S′K = CK

Let V = dP/dt, with normalized travel t = s/R. In curved space, B(P,V) = 0 and B(V,V) = K; in flat space, V = (0,v), |v| = 1.

P(s)=CK(t)P0+SK(t)V0V(s)=−KSK(t)P0+CK(t)V0

This advances position and direction analytically. No integration steps are needed between events. Curvature-dependent trigonometric functions also appear in Herranz & Ballesteros; the exact conventions above are fixed by the geometry contract.

From P₀ = (1,0,0,0), V₀ = (0,1,0,0), travel R: hyperbolic P = (cosh 1,sinh 1,0,0); spherical P = (cos 1,sin 1,0,0). Flat travel 1 gives (1,1,0,0).

2. Quaternion-pair isometries

Camera and object maps share one algebra. Let a,b,c,d be ordinary quaternions and ε a commuting scalar with ε² = K. Write g = a + εb as (a,b).

(a,b)(c,d) = (ac + Kbd, ad + bc)
|a|² + K|b|² = 1,   a·b = 0
g⁻¹ = (ā,b̄),   g† = (ā,−b̄)

Embed P = (w,X) as q(P) = ((w,0),(0,X)). The point and tangent action is q ↦ gqg†. Quaternion conjugation is marked with a bar; g† is distinct from the inverse.

translation(n,s) = (CK(s/2R), n SK(s/2R))
rotation(n,θ) = ((cos(θ/2), n sin(θ/2)), 0)

Here n is a unit pure quaternion in the translation expression. Composition is outer × inner; g and −g act identically. This framework is described by Arizmendi & Pérez-de la Rosa.

With camera pose C and object pose O, the CPU prepares C⁻¹O in f64 before checked f32 upload. Rays start at the canonical origin; canonical world maps stay unchanged during camera motion.

3. Charts and tangent frames

The hyperboloid is used for tracing. Poincaré ball and upper-half-space coordinates remain useful local charts, especially for tilings.

b = X/(w+1),   |b| < 1
h = (Xx, Xy, 1)/(w−Xz),   hz > 0
P(h) = ((|h|²+1)/(2hz), hx/hz, hy/hz, (|h|²−1)/(2hz))
ds² = 4R² |db|²/(1−|b|²)² = R² |dh|²/hz²

Changing chart does not change geometry. A unit coordinate offset in half-space has a different physical size at different heights.

Materials operate in a 3D orthonormal tangent frame. A local unit direction d becomes:

SpaceFP(d)
Euclidean(0,d)
Hyperbolic(X·d, d + X(X·d)/(w+1))
SphericalP ⊗ (0,d), interpreting P as a unit quaternion

The spherical frame is defined at antipodes too. Reflection, refraction, and diffuse sampling use local Euclidean dot products; the outgoing tangent is then transported back into the tracing frame.

4. Surface intersections

Transform the ray into each object’s local frame, solve its surface equation, and select the least valid forward distance. Built-in curved surfaces reduce to one section equation:

a CK(t) + b SK(t) = c,   s = Rt
Local surfaceEquation
Geodesic planez = 0
Curved sphere, radius rw = CK(r/R)
Hyperbolic horospherew − z = 1
Euclidean sphere|X|² = r²; solve the usual quadratic
Euclidean cubeIntersect six bounded plane faces
K = 0:   t = (c−a)/b
K = −1:   (a+b)u² − 2cu + (a−b) = 0,   u = eᵗ > 0
K = +1:   t = φ ± arccos(c/A) + 2πn,
 A = √(a²+b²),   φ = atan2(b,a)

The implementation handles degeneracies and stable root evaluation. Roots belong to [minimum, maximum); a coplanar ray has no isolated plane crossing. A tangent sphere contact may be a valid root.

The previous leaf gets a small positive minimum distance to suppress immediate self-hits. Its later intersections remain eligible. A numerical failure is a separate result from a miss and stops the path without adding environment light.

5. Spherical circuits and distance

P(s + 2πR) = P(s),   V(s + 2πR) = V(s)
sevent = R(φ + 2πn),   n = ceil((smin/R − φ)/(2π))

Here φ is a candidate phase modulo 2π. Choose n so the lifted candidate lies in the query interval. Coordinates can repeat; travelled distance cannot be reduced modulo a circuit.

At R = 1, a flight of 4π + 0.2 ends at phase 0.2. For extinction σt = 0.1, survival is e−0.1(4π+0.2) ≈ 0.279, not e−0.02 ≈ 0.980.

Opaque surfaces intercept the first eligible crossing. Longer flights arise on unobstructed paths or through repeated transmission and scattering events.

6. Intrinsic surface tilings

Surface curvature determines the pattern’s domain, independently of the surrounding space.

A ball of geodesic radius r has intrinsic sphere radius a = R SK(r/R), or a = r in flat space. Its chart is the unit spatial direction X/|X|. The spherical plane uses its unit (w,x,y) coordinates.

regular {p,q}:   1/p + 1/q > 1/2

Pattern evaluation

square chart:   g = (x,y)/cell,   tile = floor(g)
distance to nearest grid edge = cell · min(fract(g), 1−fract(g))

For squares, compare either coordinate’s edge distance with the border half-width. Hexagons use three linear edge forms in lattice coordinates; the shader scales the threshold by their gradient magnitude, 4/(3·cell), to keep the border width physical.

Hyperbolic pentagons fold the hit into a reference cell using embedded isometries. Half-space coordinates classify its sides. For side normal e and boundary offset l, the border test is e·hxy > l − width·hz; star patterns add internal side tests and parity changes. The height factor accounts for the chart’s local metric scale.

Regular spherical tiles are Voronoi regions of face-center directions: select the largest dot product. Great-circle bisectors define their borders. Lunes use meridians; dihedra add an equatorial division.

Flat cell sizes and border half-widths are physical lengths; hyperbolic pentagon widths are curvature-normalized; spherical border widths are angles in radians. Map the whole object to carry its material chart; mapping only its shape keeps the outer material frame.

7. Camera and sampling

A pinhole camera looks along local −z. For pixel (i,j), image W×H, independent uniform offsets ξx,ξy ∈ [0,1):

x = (2(i+ξx)−W)/H,   y = (H−2(j+ξy))/H
d = normalize(x, y, −1/f),   vertical FOV = 2 arctan f
P₀ = (1,0,0,0),   V₀ = (0,d)

Pixel jitter performs stochastic antialiasing. Each pixel keeps its own pseudorandom state across dispatches. Translation uses physical distances and the scene radius; camera movement resets accumulated samples.

8. Light transport

Paths start at the camera and follow geodesics between events. At a surface, the rendering equation is evaluated in its local tangent frame:

Lo=Le+∫Ωfr(ωi,ωo)Li(ωi)|n·ωi|dωi

Maintain RGB throughput β and accumulated radiance L. A sampled direction with density p updates β by fr|n·ω|/p; an emitter contributes βLe. See PBRT’s path tracing chapter for the general estimator.

A miss adds background radiance; a black background adds zero. Reaching the finite event budget returns emission collected so far. Both surface and volume events consume that budget.

Why curvature changes illumination

area of radius-r wavefront = 4π [R SK(r/R)]²
flat limit:   4πr²

Hyperbolic ray bundles spread faster; spherical bundles refocus at antipodes. The sampler follows that geometry through its rays. It does not multiply transported radiance by an extra inverse-square factor.

The current integrator samples camera paths only: no explicit light sampling, multiple importance sampling, bidirectional paths, or Russian roulette. Small emitters and caustics therefore converge slowly.

For refraction, orient unit n against incoming unit d, let c = −d·n and η = nin/nout:

k = 1 − η²(1−c²)
d′ = ηd + (ηc − √k)n   when k > 0
k ≤ 0: total internal reflection

The shader uses a small numerical band near k = 0. Its glass model applies Snell refraction or total internal reflection, without Fresnel probabilities, an η² radiance correction, or a stack of nested media. Colored glass tints each interface interaction.

Wrappers execute in nesting order. Emission outside a color wrapper is added before that color; emission inside is affected by it. Mixtures retain their own random draws; their weights describe the material mixture itself.

10. Scattering fog

The scene-wide homogeneous medium has extinction σt ≥ 0 (inverse physical length) and RGB scattering albedo ω ∈ [0,1]³.

σs = ωσt,   σa = (1−ω)σt
T(s) = exp(−σts),   p(s) = σt exp(−σts)
sfog=−ln(1−u)σt,0<u<1

If sfog < ssurface, advance to the volume event, multiply β by ω, and sample a uniform unit-sphere direction. The isotropic phase function and direction density are both 1/(4π), so their ratio is one.

Pr(no volume event before s) = exp(−σts) = T(s)

Survival is already represented by the sampled free flight. Multiplying by T again would count attenuation twice. A surface miss gives an unbounded medium interval, including in spherical space.

σt = 0: vacuum. ω = 0: pure absorption. ω > 0 with a nearby emitter: scattered glow. No emitters and a black background: black, regardless of fog density.

There is no fog emission. The current medium is homogeneous, isotropic, and also present inside glass; per-object medium boundaries are not implemented. PBRT’s volume integrators cover more general media and sampling methods.

11. Scene compilation and GPU execution

Typed Rust components (geometry G preserved)
  → validate every instance and encode parameter words
  → link component-owned shader dependencies
  → compile WGSL pipeline + upload scene buffers
  → 8 × 8 compute workgroups: sample paths per pixel
  → accumulate linear RGB and sample count
  → divide by count, apply display gamma, present

Shapes and materials provide their own shader modules and parameter encoders. The compiler deduplicates shader structure; values and vector lengths stay data. A new component follows the same extension path as the built-ins. GPU dispatch IDs are compiled data.

At each event the renderer currently scans the scene’s objects; there is no spatial acceleration structure. Camera-relative maps, material records, parameter words, pixel sums, and random states live in GPU buffers.

L̄N = (1/N) Σi=1…N Li
display = clamp(max(0,L̄N)1/2.2, 0, 1)
Monte Carlo error ∝ 1/√N   (finite-variance regime)

Linear samples are averaged before display conversion. There is no denoiser or tone mapper. Four times as many samples roughly halves sampling noise, while camera movement discards the previous view’s accumulation.

12. Precision and limits

  • f64 construction, f32 tracing. Camera-relative composition improves nearby precision. Very large hyperbolic coordinates still lose accuracy through cancellation.
  • Forward travel. Distance uses 1024-unit blocks plus a remainder. Exact block increments below 2³⁴ world units do not imply accurate geometry over that range.
  • Resolved roots. Tangency classification uses a coefficient-scaled error band. Tiny spheres or unresolved tile coordinates are rejected or terminate the sample.
  • Finite paths. Event-budget and numerical termination introduce bias. More samples reduce noise, not that bias.
  • Finite storage. Headless renders must fit device storage-binding limits. Windowed viewers can reduce render resolution and scale presentation.

The geometry contract records numerical envelopes and invariants; development notes list validation procedures and outstanding limitations.
